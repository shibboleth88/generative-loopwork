# Rules, v3 — read off the photographs at magnification

Superseded versions are in RULES-ARCHIVE.md, unedited. Crops cited below are in
`evidence/`; every claim names the crop it came from.

## The correction that forced this rewrite

**v1 and v2 said this was an alternating square-knot macrame net. It is not.**
I never looked closely; I recognised "cord, wooden rings, fringe" and supplied a
macrame vocabulary from memory, then did arithmetic on top of it. Three of the
four load-bearing rules were inventions.

At magnification there are **no square knots anywhere in either photograph** —
no filler pair with a working pair wrapping around it, which is the one thing a
square knot cannot hide. What is there instead is a **chain of loops**: a bight
of cord pulled through the previous bight, over and over. `p1_chain_tight.jpg`
shows it in heavy rope with no ambiguity at all — the interlocking V-fronts of a
chain stitch. This is crochet-family looping, done by hand at rope gauge, not
knotting.

## The unit act

**U — Make a bight in the working strand, pass it through an open loop, and let
the new bight become the open loop.**

One act. Everything below is a choice of *what you pass it through*. That choice
is the whole rule set.

## The rules

**L1 — Pass through the loop you just made → a chain.**
The default. Produces a running chain. (high — `p1_chain_tight.jpg`,
`p1_body.jpg`; the chain is unmistakable at rope gauge)

**L2 — Two strands, alternating every stitch.**
Black and cream loops are the same size, the same stitch, and strictly
alternate: a cream bight through a black bight through a cream bight. Two
strands are carried and they take turns making the stitch. (high —
`p2_colour_raw.jpg`)

> This replaces v1/v2's R5, which had colour set by the order cords were mounted
> around a ring and the pattern "emerging" from knot alternation. There is no
> mounting order. Colour alternates at the stitch, period 2, and that is all.

**L3 — Pass through an older loop → the chain joins to itself and becomes a mesh.**
A chain alone is one-dimensional. The fabric is two-dimensional, and loops are
visible with more than one strand drawn through them. (medium-high —
`p2_knotfield.jpg`, `p2_topedge.jpg`; the fabric is certainly a mesh, but which
older loop gets chosen, and how often, I cannot read off a photograph)

**L4 — Pass through a wooden ring → the ring is captured, permanently.**
Draw the bight through the ring and make the next stitch, and the ring can never
come off. **This is why rings accumulate.** (high, and it is the strongest
finding here)

> Every ring in both photographs is **empty inside** and held at **one point**.
> `p2_lowleftring.jpg` and `p1_topring.jpg` show rings dangling from a single
> cord with clear air through the middle. `p2_rightring.jpg` shows one cord
> making a U-turn through a ring. There is no lark's-head collar on any ring in
> either photograph.
>
> So v1's R1/R2 are refuted: rings are not mounting bars carrying twenty cords,
> and nothing is lark's-head mounted. They are beads threaded onto the path of
> the work. v2's R9/R10 — band outgrows ring, so it splits across two rings —
> is refuted with them: there are no bands, and the ring plays no part in
> gathering anything. The *phenomenon* the maker described is real and L4
> explains it far better: a ring joins the object whenever the rule offers a
> ring as the thing to pass through, and from then on it is structural.

**L9 — The act is worked at a tension that drifts.**
In `evidence/q_ring_meet.jpg` the *same tan cord* runs as a tight dense chain
that reads as a cable in one part of the frame and as big open loops in
another — roughly a 2.5-3x range in stitch size, in one photograph, at one
scale. The variation comes in passages, not stitch by stitch: the hand tightens
for a while and then loosens. (medium-high — the size difference is plain; that
it drifts in runs rather than randomly is inference from how it clusters)

> This is the thing the first eight rules missed. They describe *what* the act
> is and *what it passes through*, and assume one size throughout. A single
> loop size makes an even fabric, and the source is not even.

**L5 — The same act at more than one gauge.**
Heavy tan rope (`p1_body.jpg`) and fine cotton (`p2_colour_raw.jpg`) are chained
identically, at perhaps 4x difference in diameter. (high)

**L6 — Stop, cut, let the tails hang.**
Fringe is unworked strand, cut unevenly, ends combed out. (high —
`p1_fringehead.jpg`)

**L8 — The act needs an open loop and somewhere to put the bight. When the tip
has neither, the work is carried; it ends only if nowhere has room.**
Cord is unlimited. The unit act can be refused two ways: no **open loop** (one
under `loop_capacity`), or no **free site** for the bight. When the working tip
is refused, the work is not over — the loop is **carried round** to another open
loop that still has room, leaving a float across the gap. The work is over only
when no open loop anywhere will take it. (medium — the carry is forced by the
craft: cord is flexible and a working loop is never buried, you turn the piece
and keep going. That the maker stopped for a structural reason at all, rather
than simply deciding he was done, remains unevidenced.)

And of the free sites the hand tries, it uses the one **most in contact with
the fabric already made**: the act happens at the edge of the work, not off in
space. This is why the piece is one mass rather than a line, which is the most
obvious single fact about both photographs. Measured: with contact off the work
comes out a travelling rope, aspect 11.0; with it on, aspect 1.3-1.5.

> Loop capacity is set from the photographs: loops visibly carry up to about
> three strands, and in the densest regions (`evidence/p2_occ_a.jpg`) the
> apertures are closed to slivers. 2-4 is the defensible range. 3 is also the
> last value that binds — above 5, occupancy never reaches the limit and only
> the space constraint is doing any work.

> **Made faithful, this rule stops nothing.** Measured across eight very
> different settings, only the degenerate `loop_capacity=1` case ever saturates;
> every other configuration runs until the budget is spent. The reason is
> geometric: the surface of a growing mass outruns the filling of it, so there
> is always somewhere left to work. So the honest position is that **the rules
> do not end the piece — the maker does.** How much work gets done is an input
> and is now labelled as one, instead of being disguised as a consequence.

**L7 — Three-dimensionality is a consequence, not a step — and it happens
while the work is being made, not afterwards.**
A chain that keeps joining back into itself (L3) while locking rigid rings into
its own path (L4) cannot be laid flat. Nothing in this object was shaped; it
bunched. (medium — the mechanism follows from L3+L4, but I have not handled the
piece)

## Correction: the rings were the wrong colour and the wrong shape

I rendered the rings as mid-tan wood, `(0.84, 0.72, 0.52)`, and as thin hoops.
Both are wrong, and measurement off photo 2 says so plainly:

| | measured | was | now |
|---|---|---|---|
| ring colour | RGB (190, 169, 134), **brighter than the cream cord** at (142, 136, 128) | (0.84, 0.72, 0.52) | **(0.98, 0.88, 0.71)** |
| outer diameter / cord diameter | **8.7** | 10.7 | 8.7 |
| tube diameter / cord diameter | **2.4** | 1.5 | 2.4 |

So the real rings are slightly *smaller* than I had them but much *fatter*, and
near-white rather than brown. Mine read as thin wire hoops lost in the mass;
the source's are chunky, pale and visually dominant. The error was mine twice
over: I called them "beech/birch" from the look of one backlit photograph in
warm light, and never measured either quantity.

## A suspected further binding, now REFUTED

I thought `evidence/q_fringe_head.jpg` might show a cord **wrapped or coiled
around a bundle** at top right — a gathering collar, which would have been a
genuinely different binding. It is not one.

The region is backlit to silhouette, so I lifted it with a gamma of 0.22 plus
local contrast (`evidence/q_fringe_head_lifted.jpg`). That resolves two things:
a bight seen edge-on with its aperture dark, and below it a banded, tapering
cylinder — the banding being what had looked like wrapping turns.

**The measurement that killed it.** If those bands are turns of a wrapping cord,
their spacing is that cord's diameter. If they are the helical ply of a single
3-ply cord, they are a fraction of it. Autocorrelation along the feature gives a
period of **7 px** (7-8 across rows). The same measurement run on cords I can
verify are single hanging tails gives **6-9 px**. They are indistinguishable.

A wrap would also need a wrapping cord about 7 px across, and the finest cord
anywhere in the piece measures **14-20 px**. No such cord exists in the object.

So: the banding is the twist of the cord itself, and the top-right feature is
the head of the fringe — an open bight with plied cords leaving it. **No
additional binding.** The same reasoning applies to the heavy tan "rope": its
surface banding is at cord-ply scale, not bundle scale.

## Open — not refuted, not established
- **Which** older loop L3 chooses, and how often. This is the one free parameter
  with real expressive consequence and I cannot read it off a still photograph.
- Whether the maker works with a hook, a finger, or a loop of the hand.
- Whether more than two strands are ever carried.
- Whether ring capture (L4) happens on a rule or on a whim.

Answering the first and last of these needs the maker, or video, not better
crops. They are the things worth asking him.


> **Settling is interleaved with growth.** The fabric drapes under its own
> weight continuously, so each new stitch is placed onto work that has already
> moved — not onto a rigid scaffold that is relaxed once at the end. The
> generator now settles every 25 stitches and the growth tests free space
> against the settled positions. In the viewer each loop both travels and turns
> with the chain as the form moves. This is both the more faithful account and the
> reason the overall form visibly unfolds and twists as it grows: measured,
> stitches sit a mean of 3.2 stitch-pitches away from where they will end up
> when they are first made, converging monotonically to zero.
